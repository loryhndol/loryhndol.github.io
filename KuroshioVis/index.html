KuroshioVis: Hypothesis-Driven Discovery of Mesoscale Air-Sea Interaction through Coarse-to-Fine Exploration

Yuanzhi Chen1 Yiwei Lin1 Xi Duan1 Qingqing He1 Ruiliang Xu1 Yucheng Wang2 Qiong Zeng1

1Shandong University 2Laoshan Laboratory

Abstract:

Mesoscale air-sea interaction occurs throughout strong sea surface temperature fronts such as the Kuroshio Extension, but its strength is highly non-uniform and can be resolved only in kilometre-scale coupled simulations. The response of any single eddy is weak relative to synoptic variability, so evidence supporting a hypothesis of systematic air-sea coupling must be accumulated across many eddies rather than inferred from individual cases. We present KuroshioVis, a hypothesis-driven, coarse-to-fine visual analytics workflow. The coarse stage extracts and aligns mesoscale eddies into composite representations of their air-sea responses, and provides a global view for exploration. The fine stage investigates these responses through three coordinated views: the composite interface view, the cross-interface flow view, and the evidence matrix View. These coordinated views support air-sea coupling analysis from surface patterns to cross-interface dynamics and quantitative statistical evidence. We demonstrate the workflow using the coupled C1440-LLC2160 simulation of the 2026 SciVis Contest.
